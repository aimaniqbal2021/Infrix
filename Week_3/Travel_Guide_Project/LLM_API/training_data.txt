DESTINATIONS

Pakistan is home to some of the most breathtaking destinations in the world. The Hunza Valley in Gilgit-Baltistan is famous for its towering peaks, ancient forts, and apricot orchards. Skardu serves as the gateway to K2 and is known for Shangrila Resort and Upper Kachura Lake. Naran and Saif-ul-Malik Lake attract visitors with turquoise glacial water surrounded by mountains. Swat Valley, often called the "Switzerland of Pakistan," offers lush green valleys, rivers, and ski resorts in Malam Jabba. Murree is a popular hill station near Islamabad, ideal for short family trips. Lahore is known for the Badshahi Mosque, Lahore Fort, and vibrant food street culture. Islamabad combines modern city life with nearby trails such as Margalla Hills and Daman-e-Koh viewpoint. Azad Kashmir features Neelum Valley and Banjosa Lake, both popular for scenic beauty.

ACCOMMODATION

Travelers can choose from a wide range of accommodation depending on budget and destination. Budget travelers often stay in guesthouses or hostels which cost significantly less than hotels and are common in tourist towns like Hunza and Skardu. Mid-range hotels offer private rooms, attached bathrooms, and breakfast, and are widely available in cities and major tourist valleys. Luxury resorts, such as Shangrila Resort in Skardu or Serena Hotels in Gilgit and Hunza, provide premium service, scenic views, and full amenities. It is recommended to book accommodation in advance during peak tourist season (May to September) as popular destinations get fully booked. Camping is also a popular option in areas like Fairy Meadows and Deosai National Park for travelers seeking a closer connection to nature.

TRANSPORT

Getting to northern tourist destinations typically involves a mix of flights and road travel. PIA and private airlines operate flights to Gilgit and Skardu from Islamabad, though flights are weather-dependent and can be delayed or cancelled. By road, the Karakoram Highway connects Islamabad to Gilgit and Hunza, a scenic but long journey of around 18-20 hours. Local transport within valleys is usually by jeep, especially for rugged terrain like Fairy Meadows, Deosai, and Khunjerab Pass, since regular cars cannot manage those roads. Within cities, ride-hailing apps, taxis, and rickshaws are common. For Murree and Swat from Islamabad or Lahore, private cars, vans, and rented cars are the most practical option, with travel times of roughly 2-5 hours depending on traffic and the specific destination.

FOOD

Pakistani cuisine varies by region and is a major part of the travel experience. In the north, travelers often try Chapshuro (a meat-filled pastry from Hunza), apricot-based dishes, and fresh trout fish from mountain streams. Lahore is famous for its food street culture, offering items like Lahori Chargha, Nihari, and Karahi. Peshawar is known for Chapli Kabab and Namkeen Karahi. Karachi offers excellent seafood and the famous Karachi Biryani. Street food across Pakistan commonly includes Samosas, Pakoras, and Chaat, especially popular in the evenings and during Ramadan. Tea (chai) is a staple offered almost everywhere, including remote mountain villages, often as a gesture of hospitality.

GENERAL TRAVEL TIPS

The best time to visit northern Pakistan is between May and September when mountain passes are open and weather is mild; winters bring heavy snowfall that can close roads like the Babusar Pass. Travelers should carry warm clothing even in summer, as temperatures drop significantly at night in high-altitude areas. A valid CNIC or passport may be required for checkpoints in certain regions such as Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir. It's advisable to carry cash, as ATMs are limited in remote valleys. Mobile network coverage can be unreliable in mountainous areas, so travelers should inform someone of their itinerary in advance. Respecting local customs and dress codes, especially in rural and religious areas, is appreciated by local communities.

CULTURE AND FESTIVALS

Pakistan's culture varies widely by region. In Hunza and Gilgit-Baltistan, the Shandur Polo Festival is held every July at the "Roof of the World," the highest polo ground in the world, drawing teams and spectators from across the region. Basant, a kite-flying festival, was historically celebrated in Lahore, though it is now restricted in many areas. Eid-ul-Fitr and Eid-ul-Adha are the two biggest national celebrations, marked by family gatherings, special meals, and decorated markets in the weeks beforehand. In the north, Kalash communities in Chitral celebrate unique festivals such as Chilam Joshi in spring, featuring traditional dance, music, and dress distinct from the rest of the country. Truck art, visible across highways including the Karakoram Highway, is considered a recognizable part of Pakistani visual culture and is often photographed by tourists.

SHOPPING AND SOUVENIRS

Popular souvenirs from northern Pakistan include hand-knitted Hunza caps (locally called "Hunza topi"), dried fruits such as apricots and mulberries, and local gemstones including topaz and aquamarine sold in Gilgit and Hunza markets. Lahore's Anarkali Bazaar and Liberty Market are well known for traditional clothing, embroidered shawls, and jewelry. Peshawar's old bazaars are known for leather goods, carpets, and dry fruits. When shopping in local bazaars, bargaining is common and often expected, especially for handicrafts and souvenirs rather than fixed-price goods in malls. Truck art-themed items, such as miniature decorated trucks and bags, have also become popular novelty souvenirs for tourists.

SAFETY AND PERMITS

Most tourist areas in northern Pakistan are open to domestic and foreign travelers without special permits, but certain border-adjacent zones, such as parts of Khunjerab National Park near the China border and some areas of Azad Kashmir near the Line of Control, may require a No Objection Certificate (NOC) or are subject to checkpoints. Foreign tourists are generally advised to register at checkpoints when traveling through Gilgit-Baltistan, as this is a standard local procedure rather than a sign of danger. Hiring a local guide for trekking routes such as Fairy Meadows, Rakaposhi Base Camp, or Deosai is recommended, both for safety on unmarked trails and for support with the local language. Travel insurance covering high-altitude trekking is recommended for activities above 3,000 meters.

CURRENCY AND BUDGETING

The local currency is the Pakistani Rupee (PKR). Major cities have ATMs, but remote valleys such as Hunza's upper villages or Fairy Meadows often have limited or no ATM access, so carrying sufficient cash in advance is important. Budget travelers can expect daily costs (food, local transport, guesthouse stay) to be considerably lower than in Western countries, while mid-range and luxury travelers will pay more for hotels like Serena or Shangrila resorts. Entry fees apply at some national parks, including Deosai National Park and Khunjerab National Park, typically charged per person and per vehicle. Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory in most guesthouses and local restaurants.
